This dissertation investigates the thermo-hydro-mechanical (THM) behavior of concrete exposed to high temperatures, with a particular focus on pore pressure development under conditions representative of severe thermal loading.

The work is based on the implementation and calibration of a coupled THM model in Cast3M. Numerical simulations are performed using the experimental campaign conducted by Kalifa et al. as a reference benchmark. The study aims to reproduce the measured temperature and gas pressure evolutions within concrete specimens and to evaluate the capability of the model to capture the underlying transport and coupling mechanisms.

A calibration procedure is developed to identify the influence of key material and environmental parameters on the numerical response. Particular attention is given to the initial moisture state of the concrete, represented through the degree of saturation and relative humidity, as these parameters strongly affect pore pressure generation. To facilitate the calibration process, Python scripts are developed to automate simulation runs and the extraction of relevant results.

The numerical results are compared with experimental measurements at different depths within the concrete specimen. The study discusses the strengths and limitations of the current model and provides insights into the sensitivity of the predicted response to the selected parameters. The outcomes contribute to the assessment and validation of advanced THM modelling approaches for concrete subjected to elevated temperatures.




This thesis investigates the thermo-hydro-mechanical (THM) behavior of High-Performance Concrete (HPC) under severe accident conditions using an advanced poro-mechanical approach. Utilizing the Cast3M simulation environment, the study combines numerical modeling with direct experimental validation—benchmarked against Dauti’s thesis data—to better understand the complex, coupled physics involved. A critical focus of this research is the transition from standard hydration to high-temperature dehydration, accurately tracking chemical damage and structural micro-cracking during thermal ramps. By systematically calibrating surface boundary conditions (such as emissivity and convective exchange coefficients) alongside the material's temperature-dependent intrinsic permeability, the model captures the internal gas pressure build-up at varying depths. Furthermore, the deterministic THM simulations are coupled with a probabilistic framework—employing FORM, SORM, and Monte Carlo algorithms—to rigorously evaluate the risk of spalling induced by pore pressure and the localized degradation of tensile strength.
