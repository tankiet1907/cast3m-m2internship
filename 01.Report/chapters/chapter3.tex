This chapter describes the numerical solution of the THM governing equations developed in \cref{chapter2}. The implementation proceeds incrementally: the thermal, hydric, and mechanical models are first solved separately, then coupled to form the full THM system. All implementations are carried out in the finite element code Cast3M.

\section{Discretization}\label{sec:discretization}

\subsection{Spatial discretization} \label{sec:spatial_discret}

\begin{reviewblock}
The specific coupling method mentioned in \cref{sec:mechanical} between the TH and mechanical solvers has not yet been finalized and will be updated in \cref{chapter2} and \cref{chapter3} as the study progresses.
\end{reviewblock}

Applying the standard Galerkin finite element method to the three TH conservation equations (\cref{eq:final_air,eq:final_water,eq:final_energy}) yields the semi-discrete system \cite{dauti2018}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{C}\,\dot{\mathbf{Y}} + \mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{f}
    \label{eq:TH_matrix}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{Y} = \{\bar{p}_g,\, \bar{p}_c,\, \bar{T}\}$ is the vector of nodal TH unknowns. The operators $\mathbf{C}$ (capacity), $\mathbf{K}$(conductivity), and $\mathbf{f}$ (forcing) are $3\times3$ block matrices:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K} = \begin{pmatrix}
    K_{gg} & K_{gc} & K_{gt} \\
    K_{cg} & K_{cc} & K_{ct} \\
    K_{tg} & K_{tc} & K_{tt}
    \end{pmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{C} = \begin{pmatrix}
    C_{gg} & C_{gc} & C_{gt} \\
    0      & C_{cc} & C_{ct} \\
    0      & C_{tc} & C_{tt}
    \end{pmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{f} = \begin{pmatrix}
    f_g \\ f_c \\ f_t
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:TH_blocks}
\end{equation}
The off-diagonal entries $K_{ij}$, $C_{ij}$ ($i\neq j$) encode the coupling between the thermal and hydric fields. Detailed expressions of each block are given in \cite{dauti2018}, Appendix~A.

The mechanical problem (\cref{eq:final_momentum}) is solved separately in a staggered manner: at each time step, the converged TH solution $\bar{T}$, $\bar{p}_g$, $\bar{p}_c$ enters the mechanical problem as prescribed loading through the two mechanisms defined in \cref{sec:mechanical} --- strain decomposition (\cref{eq:strain_decomp}) and effective stress (\cref{eq:eff_stress}). The discrete equilibrium (\cref{eq:KMU_F,eq:F_full}) reduces to solving:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K}_M\,\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{F}
    \label{eq:mech_matrix}
\end{equation}
for the nodal displacement vector $\mathbf{U}$, where $\mathbf{F}$ is the load vector defined in \cref{eq:F_full}.

\subsection{Temporal discretization} \label{sec:temporal_discret}
\review{This section is currently under development and will be refined as the study progresses.}

The time integration of \cref{eq:TH_matrix} is carried out using the Wilson-$\theta$ method \cite{dauti2018}, which evaluates the equation at an intermediate time $t_{n+\theta} = t_n + \theta\,\Delta t$ with $\theta \in [0,1]$. This transforms the semi-discrete system into a fully discrete algebraic system at each time step:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{K}}\,\Delta\mathbf{Y}^{n+1} 
    + \mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{Y}^{n} 
    = \mathbf{f}^{n+\theta}
    \label{eq:theta_method}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathbf{Y}^{n+1} = \mathbf{Y}^{n+1} 
    - \mathbf{Y}^{n}, \qquad
    \tilde{\mathbf{K}} = \frac{\mathbf{C}}{\Delta t} 
    + \theta\,\mathbf{K}
    \label{eq:theta_Ktilde}
\end{equation}
The parameter $\theta$ controls the scheme: $\theta=0$ gives the explicit forward Euler method, $\theta=1$ the fully implicit backward Euler, and $\theta=0.5$ the Crank-Nicolson scheme. In practice, $\theta=1$ is used for unconditional stability.

Since the capacity and conductivity matrices depend nonlinearly on the unknowns ($\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{Y})$, $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{K}(\mathbf{Y})$), \cref{eq:theta_method} is solved iteratively using the Newton-Raphson method within each time step.

\subsection{Solution strategy} \label{sec:solution_strategy}
\review{This section is currently under development and will be refined as the study progresses.}

The TH subsystem (\cref{eq:theta_method}) is solved \textit{monolithically}: all three unknowns $p_g$, $p_c$, $T$ are updated simultaneously within a single Newton-Raphson loop at each time step.

The mechanical problem (\cref{eq:mech_matrix}) is solved in a \textit{staggered} manner: at each time step, the converged TH solution provides $\bar{T}$, $\bar{p}_g$, $\bar{p}_c$, which are used to assemble $\mathbf{F}$ (\cref{eq:F_full}) and solve for $\mathbf{U}$. The damage variable $\mathcal{D}$ computed from the Mazars model feeds back to the intrinsic permeability via \cref{eq:perm_damage}, creating a weak M$\to$H coupling updated at the next time step.

\section{Implementation of the THM coupled model}
The THM coupling follows the staggered TH$\to$M approach introduced in \cref{sec:mechanical}: the TH subsystem (\cref{sec:TH_implementation}) is solved first, and the resulting fields $\bar{T}$, $\bar{p}_g$, $\bar{p}_c$ are passed as prescribed loading to the mechanical step through the load vector $\mathbf{F}$ defined in \cref{eq:F_full}.

\subsection{Thermal and shrinkage strain input}

This section is currently under development and will be refined as the study progresses.

The thermal dilation strain $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{th}$ (\cref{eq:eps_th}) and shrinkage strain $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{sh}$ (\cref{eq:eps_sh}), computed from $\bar{T}$ and $\bar{p}_c$, contribute to the load vector as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}^{th+sh} = \int_V \mathbf{B}^T \mathbf{D}_e\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{th}\,dV + \int_V \mathbf{B}^T \mathbf{D}_e\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{sh}\,dV
\end{equation}

\subsection{Pore pressure loading input}

This section is currently under development and will be refined as the study progresses.

The effective stress contribution from pore pressures $\bar{p}_g$, $\bar{p}_c$ (\cref{eq:eff_stress}) enters the load vector as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}^{pore} = \int_V \mathbf{B}^T\, \alpha_{Biot}(S_l\,p_c + p_g)\,\mathbf{I}\,dV
\end{equation}
