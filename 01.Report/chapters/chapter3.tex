\section{The Kalifa experiment} \label{sec:kalifa}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
The campaign of Kalifa et al.~\cite{kalifa2000} quantifies the \emph{thermo-hydral} process responsible for spalling. When concrete is heated on one face, free and chemically bound water are released as vapour; part migrates inward, recondenses in cooler regions and forms a quasi-saturated \emph{moisture clog} that blocks gas evacuation and triggers a sharp pore-pressure build-up behind the drying front. The test measures \emph{simultaneously and continuously} the temperature, the gas (pore) pressure at several depths, and the global mass loss, providing a quantitative dataset for validating coupled codes.
 
\paragraph{Set-up and instrumentation.}
A prismatic specimen of $30\times30\times12~\si{\centi\meter\cubed}$ is heated on one $30\times30~\si{\centi\meter\squared}$ face by a radiant heater (up to \SI{5}{\kilo\watt}, \SI{800}{\celsius}) placed \SI{3}{\centi\meter} above the surface. The lateral faces are insulated with porous ceramic blocks, so heat and mass transfer are essentially one-dimensional. The specimen rests on a balance recording its mass. Five gauges measure pressure and temperature at \SIlist{10;20;30;40;50}{\milli\meter} from the heated face, and a sixth tube measures the surface temperature at \SI{2}{\milli\meter} depth.

\paragraph{Concrete mix.}
Only the high-performance concrete (HPC, mix~M100-C) is retained for the
modelling: it exhibits the most pronounced thermo-hydral response (measured
gas-pressure peak $\approx\SI{40}{\bar}$) and an initial liquid saturation
$S_{l,0}\approx0.77$. Its mixture proportions, after Kalifa et
al.~\cite{kalifa2000}, are given in Table~\ref{tab:hpc-mix}: a CEM\,I 52.5
cement blended with silica fume at a low water/binder ratio, giving the high
compactness and low permeability responsible for the strong pressurisation. The
lumped quantities fed to Powers' model ($c$, aggregate, $w/c$, $s/c$) are those
listed in Table~\ref{tab:params}.

\paragraph{Heating scenario and measured data.}
In the experiment the specimen is heated on one face by a radiant heater applied
as a step signal, the heater temperature being set to one of
\SIlist{450;600;800}{\celsius}. The \SI{600}{\celsius} test is retained as the
reference loading, since it develops a complete pressurisation/relief cycle
while preserving the quasi-1-D transfer condition. Under this loading the
typical response combines a temperature rise with a short plateau near
\SI{200}{\celsius} (intense vaporisation), a two-stage gas-pressure history
(pressurisation, then relief as the drying front passes the gauge), and a
continuous mass loss; in HPC the pore pressure exceeds the saturating vapour
pressure $p_{vs}(T)$.

These three quantities — temperature and gas pressure at the gauge depths and
the global mass loss — are the validation targets of this work. They are not
plotted here: to keep the experimental curves together with the simulated
response, they are reported alongside the numerical results in the calibration
in \cref{sec:calibration}).

\section{Numerical model}
\label{sec:num-model}
\paragraph{Scope of the numerical simulation (THC model).} It is important to note that while the full Thermo-Hydro-Mechanical (THM) theoretical framework is derived in \cref{chapter2} and \cref{appendixA}, the numerical application in this chapter is restricted to the Thermo-Hydro-Chemical (THC) model. Because the reference Kalifa experiment only provides temperature and gas pressure data, there are no experimental mechanical counterparts (e.g., strain or damage measurements) available to validate the structural response. Consequently, the staggered mechanical module---though formulated as an easily pluggable component---is kept out of the current simulation scope.

\paragraph{Geometry and mesh.}
The test is reproduced by a two-dimensional plane model of the
$120\times120~\si{\milli\meter\squared}$ cross-section
(Figure~\ref{fig:setup}), with the heated (hot) face at the top and the cold
face at the bottom; the two lateral faces are thermally and hydrically
insulated, so the model reproduces the quasi-1-D transfer of the experiment.
The domain is discretised with $24\times24$ four-noded quadrilateral (QUA4)
elements (the reference paper used $28\times28$~\cite{dauti2018}). Field
histories are extracted along the central vertical line ($x=\SI{60}{\milli\meter}$)
at depths of $0,10,20,30,40,50$ and \SI{120}{\milli\meter} from the hot face,
matching the gauge positions. A graded mesh refined toward the hot face
($\sim\SI{1}{\milli\meter}$) is also available for the mesh-sensitivity study.

\paragraph{Material parameters.} The reference parameters corresponding to the HPC M100 mix are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:params}. Instead of treating high-temperature concrete as a purely empirical material, the inputs feed directly into the THC constitutive laws:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hydration kinetics ($A_i, A_p, \Gamma_p, \zeta$):} These parameters define the normalized chemical affinity $\mathcal{A}(\Gamma)$ which drives the hydration rate during the curing phase. The full equation governing this behavior is detailed in \cref{sec:affinity}.
    \item \textbf{Permeability evolution ($K_0, A_\Gamma, B_K$):} Rather than applying an empirical temperature-dependent multiplier, the intrinsic permeability $K$ is explicitly coupled to the effective hydration degree $\tilde{\Gamma}$ via the relation:
    \begin{equation}
        K(\tilde{\Gamma}) = K_0 \cdot 10^{A_\Gamma (1 - \tilde{\Gamma})^{B_K}}
        \label{eq:ch3-perm}
    \end{equation}
    where $K_0$ is the initial intrinsic permeability (often denoted $K_1$ in the numerical code). $A_\Gamma$ dictates the magnitude of permeability increase as C-S-H decomposes, while $B_K$ controls the curve shape. Further theoretical derivations are provided in \cref{sec:perm-law}.
\end{itemize}
The porosity, the water content at full hydration, and the initial hydration field are internally computed using Powers' stoichiometric model (\cref{appendixA}).

\paragraph{Thermal input and boundary conditions.}
The boundary set-up follows the modelling of Dauti~\cite{dauti2018}
(Figure~\ref{fig:setup}). On the hot face a combined convection--radiation
condition is imposed,
\begin{equation}
  -\,\lambda\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial n}\Big|_{hot}
  = h^{\mathrm{hot}}\,(T_\infty - T) + \epsilon\,\sigma\,\big(T_\infty^4 - T^4\big),
  \label{eq:heated-face}
\end{equation}
with $h^{\mathrm{hot}}=\SI{8}{\watt\per\meter\squared\per\kelvin}$, emissivity
$\epsilon=0.7$, and a prescribed radiator temperature $T_\infty(t)$ that rises
to $\approx\SI{400}{\celsius}$ at the onset of the accident and then increases
quasi-linearly to $\approx\SI{466}{\celsius}$ over the $\approx\SI{240}{\minute}$
accident (Figure~\ref{fig:setup}b); the radiator is switched off afterwards.
The cold face exchanges heat by convection only
($h^{\mathrm{cold}}=\SI{8.3}{\watt\per\meter\squared\per\kelvin}$,
$T_\infty=\SI{20}{\celsius}$). On both faces the gas pressure is fixed at
atmospheric ($p_g=p_{atm}$) and a convective vapour flux
$q_w=h_g(\rho_{gw}^{s}-\rho_{gw}^{ext})$ is applied, the mass-transfer
coefficient $h_m$ increasing from $0$ (sealed) to
$\SI{2e-3}{}$ (drying) and $\SI{1.8e-2}{\meter\per\second}$ (accident).

The radiator history is adapted from Dauti~\cite{dauti2018}: rather than a raw
furnace curve, it is shaped so that the resulting \emph{outer-surface}
temperature of the specimen reproduces a physically reasonable response; the
ramp parameters are subsequently re-calibrated against the temperature
measurements in Chapter~4 (\cref{sec:cal-temp}).

\paragraph{Curing, drying and initial conditions.}
A key feature of the THC framework~\cite{sciume2024} is that the hygro-chemical
state preceding the accident is obtained from a preliminary \emph{curing}
simulation rather than prescribed arbitrarily. The computation starts from
\begin{equation}
  T_0=\SI{20}{\celsius},\quad p_{g,0}=\SI{101325}{\pascal},\quad
  \mathrm{RH}_0=0.5,\quad \Gamma_0=0,
  \label{eq:initial-conditions}
\end{equation}
the initial liquid saturation following from the desorption isotherm and
Kelvin's law. Hydration then develops over the curing period, building
self-consistent profiles of hydration degree $\Gamma$, saturation $S_l$ and
relative humidity that are carried over as initial fields for the heating
(drying) stage. The full history is run in three staged \texttt{PASAPAS}
computations: (i) \emph{early age} (0--3~d, sealed, adiabatic); (ii)
\emph{aging} (3~d--1~yr, heat and moisture convection, external humidity reduced
from $0.95$ to $0.50$); and (iii) \emph{accident} ($+\SI{240}{\minute}$,
hot-face convection and radiation). The aging stage fixes the hydro-chemical
state ($\Gamma$, $S_l$, RH) just before the accident, so that the initial water
content and hydration state — which strongly govern the pressure amplitude — are
consistent with the specimen history.

\textbf{Noted}: The value $\mathrm{RH}_0=0.5$ imposed during early age is physically
unrealistic for a sealed fresh specimen, but it is retained because higher
values cause the early-age solution to diverge. Reverting it to
$\mathrm{RH}_0=0.98$ was tested in an attempt to improve the gas-pressure
prediction; the result confirms that this initial value brings no useful
improvement at the calibration stage (\cref{sec:cal-pressure}).
 
\paragraph{Solution strategy and numerical robustness.}
\label{sec:robustness}
At high temperature, the THC system is \emph{strongly nonlinear}. The Newton iterations of the nonlinear \texttt{PASAPAS} integrator may therefore \emph{diverge}. Three measures are investigated to restore convergence:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Increasing the relaxation parameter $\theta$} toward a fully implicit time integration ($\theta=1$, used here). This scheme efficiently damps the oscillations of the nonlinear coefficients. The mathematical formulation of this $\theta$-method is detailed in \cref{sec:temporal_discret}.
  \item \textbf{Reducing the time step $\Delta t$}: from \SI{600}{\second} during aging down to \SIlist{5;15}{\second} across the thermal ramp. Furthermore, $\Delta t$ is strictly reduced to \SI{1}{\second} during the first and last minutes of the thermal ramp where temperature gradients are extremely high, thereby enlarging the convergence radius (see calibration in \cref{sec:cal-temp}).
  \item \textbf{Refining the mesh} toward the hot face (graded mesh, $\sim\SI{1}{\milli\meter}$), which limits the inter-element jumps of variables.
  \item \textbf{Tuning the thermal radiation}: the thermal radiation input (emissivity) at the heated boundary is deliberately adjusted  and reduced to prevent excessive surface overheating. $\epsilon=0.85$ in \cite{dauti2018} is reduced to $\epsilon=0.7$. This keeps the resulting temperatures within a tractable range and ensures stable code execution (for full calibration details, see \cref{sec:cal-temp}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Application of the THC model} \label{sec:thc-application}

\paragraph{Implementation in \textsc{Cast3M}.} The THC model is implemented in \textsc{Cast3M}. The coupled convective mass/heat boundary conditions are strongly nonlinear: the fluid densities entering the convective fluxes must be expressed in terms of the state variables $(p_c,p_g,T)$, which does not admit a closed algebraic form suitable for the standard discretisation. To handle these complex boundary mechanisms, the implementation relies on a \emph{custom build} of \textsc{Cast3M}~2024, or on the 2026 pre-release version developed by G.~Sciumè, S.~Dal~Pont and D.~Dauti, which provides the dedicated operators required by the THC formulation.

\paragraph{Permeability evolution with $K_1$ and $A_\Gamma$.} \label{ssec:permeability} Intrinsic permeability most strongly governs the pressure amplitude. As briefly introduced in \cref{eq:ch3-perm}, the permeability rises monotonically as $\tilde{\Gamma}$ decreases. The reference inputs $K_1=\SI{2e-19}{\meter\squared}$ (equivalent to $K_0$) and $A_\Gamma=3$ (Table~\ref{tab:params}) are the \emph{starting point} of the calibration of \cref{chapter4}, where $K_1$ and $A_\Gamma$ are the two parameters swept against the measured gas pressure. Driving transport through the same internal variable that controls porosity and sorption avoids a separate temperature-dependent multiplier. The residual post-peak pressure decay attributable to \emph{mechanical} microcracking is theoretically governed by a damage-dependent law \cite{gawin1999}; however, as the mechanical module is not engaged in this study, capturing this post-peak decay is left as a perspective for future fully-coupled THM developments.

\paragraph{Summary.} The THC application is a chain of chemo-physical effects: the curing/heating history fixes $\Gamma$ and $F$, hence $\tilde{\Gamma}$, which controls the porosity, the saturation curve and the permeability law (\cref{sec:perm-law}); these properties determine the temperature, gas-pressure and mass-loss responses compared with the Kalifa data. Calibration and validation against the experiment are presented in the next chapter.
