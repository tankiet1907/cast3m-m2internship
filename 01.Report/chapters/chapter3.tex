\chapter{Numerical implementation} \label{chapter3}

This chapter describes the numerical solution of the THM governing equations developed in \cref{chapter2}. The implementation proceeds incrementally: the thermal, hydric, and mechanical models are first solved separately, then coupled to form the full THM system. All implementations are carried out in the finite element code Cast3M.

\section{Discretization}\label{sec:discretization}

\subsection{Spatial discretization} \label{sec:spatial_discret}
\review{This section is currently under development and will be refined as the study progresses.}

Applying the standard Galerkin finite element method to the three TH conservation equations (\cref{eq:final_air,eq:final_water,eq:final_energy}) yields the semi-discrete system \cite{dauti2018}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{C}\,\dot{\mathbf{Y}} + \mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{f}
    \label{eq:TH_matrix}
\end{equation}

where $\mathbf{Y} = \{\bar{p}_g,\, \bar{p}_c,\, \bar{T}\}$ is the vector of nodal TH unknowns. The operators $\mathbf{C}$ (capacity), $\mathbf{K}$ (conductivity), and $\mathbf{f}$ (forcing) are $3\times3$ block matrices:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K} = \begin{pmatrix}
    K_{gg} & K_{gc} & K_{gt} \\
    K_{cg} & K_{cc} & K_{ct} \\
    K_{tg} & K_{tc} & K_{tt}
    \end{pmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{C} = \begin{pmatrix}
    C_{gg} & C_{gc} & C_{gt} \\
    0      & C_{cc} & C_{ct} \\
    0      & C_{tc} & C_{tt}
    \end{pmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{f} = \begin{pmatrix}
    f_g \\ f_c \\ f_t
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:TH_blocks}
\end{equation}
The off-diagonal entries $K_{ij}$, $C_{ij}$ ($i\neq j$) encode the coupling between the thermal and hydric fields. Detailed expressions of each block are given in \cite{dauti2018}, Appendix~A.

The mechanical problem (\cref{eq:final_momentum}) is solved separately in a staggered manner: at each time step, the TH solution provides $T$, $p_g$, $p_c$,which define the thermal strain (\cref{eq:eps_th}), shrinkage strain (\cref{eq:eps_sh}), and pore pressure loading (\cref{eq:eff_stress}). The equilibrium equation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K}_M\,\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{F}(T,\, p_g,\, p_c)
    \label{eq:mech_matrix}
\end{equation}
is then solved for the displacement field $\mathbf{u}$, where $\mathbf{K}_M$ is the mechanical stiffness matrix and $\mathbf{F}$ is the load vector incorporating thermo-hydric contributions.

\subsection{Temporal discretization} \label{sec:temporal_discret}
\review{This section is currently under development and will be refined as the study progresses.}

The time integration of \cref{eq:TH_matrix} is carried out using the Wilson-$\theta$ method \cite{dauti2018}, which evaluates the equation at an intermediate time $t_{n+\theta} = t_n + \theta\,\Delta t$ with $\theta \in [0,1]$. This transforms the semi-discrete system into a fully discrete algebraic system at each time step:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{K}}\,\Delta\mathbf{Y}^{n+1} 
    + \mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{Y}^{n} 
    = \mathbf{f}^{n+\theta}
    \label{eq:theta_method}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathbf{Y}^{n+1} = \mathbf{Y}^{n+1} 
    - \mathbf{Y}^{n}, \qquad
    \tilde{\mathbf{K}} = \frac{\mathbf{C}}{\Delta t} 
    + \theta\,\mathbf{K}
    \label{eq:theta_Ktilde}
\end{equation}
The parameter $\theta$ controls the scheme: $\theta=0$ gives the explicit forward Euler method, $\theta=1$ the fully implicit backward Euler, and $\theta=0.5$ the Crank-Nicolson scheme. In practice, $\theta=1$ is used for unconditional stability.

Since the capacity and conductivity matrices depend nonlinearly on the unknowns ($\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{Y})$, $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{K}(\mathbf{Y})$), \cref{eq:theta_method} is solved iteratively using the Newton-Raphson method within each time step.

\subsection{Solution strategy} \label{sec:solution_strategy}
\review{This section is currently under development and will be refined as the study progresses.}

The TH subsystem (\cref{eq:theta_method}) is solved \textit{monolithically}: all three unknowns $p_g$, $p_c$, $T$ are updated simultaneously within a single Newton-Raphson loop at each time step.

The mechanical problem (\cref{eq:mech_matrix}) is solved in a \textit{staggered} manner: at each time step, the converged TH solution provides $T$, $p_g$, $p_c$, which are then used to assemble $\mathbf{F}$ and solve for $\mathbf{u}$. The damage variable $\mathcal{D}$ computed from the Mazars model feeds back to the intrinsic permeability via \cref{eq:perm_Td}, creating a weak M$\to$H coupling that is updated at the next time step.
