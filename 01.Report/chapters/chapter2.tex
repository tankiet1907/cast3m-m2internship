\section{Thermal and hydric phenomena in heated concrete} \label{sec:phenomena}

When one face of a concrete member is exposed to a rising temperature, a sequence of strongly interacting thermal and hydric processes develops through the thickness. They are summarised here; the full constitutive relations are derived in Appendix~\ref{app:thm}.
 
\paragraph{Thermal phenomena.}
Heat is transmitted into the material essentially by \emph{conduction}, governed by Fourier's law, while \emph{convection} and \emph{radiation} act on the heated boundary and are accounted for through the boundary conditions. Energy transport is not purely conductive: the bulk flow of liquid water and gas advects enthalpy, and the phase changes (evaporation, dehydration) consume energy and locally slow the temperature rise. The resulting energy balance and the temperature dependence of the heat capacity $\rho C_p$ and conductivity $\lambda$ are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:thermal}.
 
\paragraph{Hydric phenomena.}
Concrete is treated as a multiphase porous medium made of a solid skeleton ($s$), liquid water ($l$), water vapour ($v$) and dry air ($a$). On heating, free water \emph{evaporates} and chemically bound water is released from the calcium--silicate--hydrates (C--S--H) through \emph{dehydration}. The generated vapour is transported by pressure-driven \emph{Darcy flow} and by \emph{Fickian diffusion} within the gas mixture. Part of the vapour migrates inward, reaches cooler regions and \emph{recondenses}, building a quasi-saturated layer --- the \emph{moisture clog} --- that blocks further gas transport. Behind this clog the gas pressure $p_g$ rises sharply (Section~\ref{sec:spalling}). The governing transport and mass-balance relations are given in Appendix~\ref{app:hydric}.
 
These two families of phenomena are the physical origin of the two spalling mechanisms described next.

\section{Spalling mechanisms} \label{sec:spalling}

Spalling is the violent or non-violent detachment of layers or pieces of concrete from the surface of a heated structural element. It can cause premature structural failure through reinforcement exposure and cross-section reduction, as illustrated by several catastrophic tunnel fires (Channel Tunnel 1996, Mont-Blanc 1999, St.\ Gotthard 2001) \cite{thmframework}. Despite extensive experimental work, no single theory has reached consensus; the two most widely accepted mechanisms, and their combined action, are presented below.
 
\subsection{Thermal-gradient mechanism}
A steep temperature gradient through the thickness produces differential thermal dilation:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Compressive stresses in the heated cover}: the surface layers tend to expand but are restrained by the cooler interior, generating compressive stresses parallel to the heated face.
  \item \textbf{Tensile stresses in the interior}: the restraint of the expanding surface induces tension in the deeper layers.
  \item \textbf{Fracture and energy release}: when the tensile stress exceeds the tensile strength $f_t$, cracks form parallel to the surface; the sudden release of stored elastic energy can drive the explosive ejection of fragments.
\end{enumerate}
High-performance concrete (HPC) is particularly prone to this mechanism because its higher brittleness stores more elastic energy before failure, leading to a more violent release.
 
\subsection{Pore-pressure mechanism (moisture clog)}
The sequence of events is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Dry zone}: near the heated face, free water evaporates and bound water is released by dehydration, forming a dry zone.
  \item \textbf{Vapour migration}: part of the vapour escapes towards the heated face, part is driven inward.
  \item \textbf{Condensation / moisture clog}: the inward vapour condenses where $T<T_{sat}$, forming a quasi-saturated, nearly impermeable barrier.
  \item \textbf{Pressure build-up}: behind the clog, $p_g$ rises sharply as evaporation feeds a region from which vapour cannot escape; thermal dilation of liquid water in saturated pores adds to the build-up.
  \item \textbf{Tensile failure}: when $p_g$ exceeds the tensile strength, cracks form parallel to the surface, potentially leading to explosive spalling.
\end{enumerate}
The governing parameters are the intrinsic permeability $K_0$ (low permeability hinders vapour evacuation) and the initial moisture content (more water to evaporate), both promoting higher pore pressures.
 
\subsection{Combined mechanism}
In practice, explosive spalling generally results from the \emph{combined action} of pore pressure and thermal stress. The spalling condition can be written schematically as
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{thermal}(T) + \sigma_{pore}(p_g,p_c,S_l) \ge f_t(T),
  \label{eq:spalling}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{thermal}$ is the thermal stress from differential dilation, $\sigma_{pore}$ the stress from pore-pressure loading (via the effective stress, Section~\ref{sec:mech}), and $f_t(T)$ the temperature-degraded tensile strength. Equation~\eqref{eq:spalling} makes clear why a complete coupled model is needed: neither a purely thermal nor a purely hydric analysis can capture the full picture.
    

\section{Thermo-hydric (TH) coupled model} \label{sec:thmodel}

The thermal and hydric fields are described by three primary variables,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{U}_{TH} = \{\, p_g,\; p_c,\; T \,\},
\end{equation}
the gas pressure, the capillary pressure and the temperature. Each is associated with a conservation equation. After substitution of Darcy's law, Fick's law and the constitutive relations (Appendix~\ref{app:hydric}), the final coupled forms read:
 
\paragraph{Dry-air conservation.}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\phi(1-S_l)\!\left(\frac{\partial\rho_a}{\partial T}\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial\rho_a}{\partial p_c}\frac{\partial p_c}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial\rho_a}{\partial p_g}\frac{\partial p_g}{\partial t}\right)
+(1-S_l)\rho_a\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial T}\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} \\
&-\phi\rho_a\!\left(\frac{\partial S_l}{\partial p_c}\frac{\partial p_c}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial S_l}{\partial T}\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}\right)
-\nabla\!\cdot\!\left(\frac{K\rho_a k_{rg}}{\mu_g}\nabla p_g\right)
-\nabla\!\cdot\!\left(D\rho_g\frac{M_v M_a}{M_g^2}\nabla\frac{p_a}{p_g}\right)=0 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:air}
\end{equation}
 
\paragraph{Water-species conservation.}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\big(S_l\rho_l-(1-S_l)\rho_v\big)\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial T}\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
+S_l\phi\frac{\partial\rho_l}{\partial T}\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
+\phi(\rho_l-\rho_v)\!\left(\frac{\partial S_l}{\partial T}\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial S_l}{\partial p_c}\frac{\partial p_c}{\partial t}\right) \\
&+(1-S_l)\phi\!\left(\frac{\partial\rho_v}{\partial T}\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial\rho_v}{\partial p_c}\frac{\partial p_c}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial\rho_v}{\partial p_g}\frac{\partial p_g}{\partial t}\right)
-\nabla\!\cdot\!\left(\frac{K\rho_l k_{rl}}{\mu_l}(\nabla p_g-\nabla p_c)\right) \\
&-\nabla\!\cdot\!\left(\frac{K\rho_v k_{rg}}{\mu_g}\nabla p_g\right)
-\nabla\!\cdot\!\left(D\rho_g\frac{M_v M_a}{M_g^2}\nabla\frac{p_v}{p_g}\right)
= -\dot{m}_{dehyd} .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:water}
\end{equation}
 
\paragraph{Energy conservation.}
\begin{equation}
\rho C_p\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
-K\!\left(C_l\frac{\rho_l k_{rl}}{\mu_l}(\nabla p_g-\nabla p_c)
+C_g\frac{\rho_v k_{rg}}{\mu_g}\nabla p_g\right)\!\cdot\!\nabla T
-\nabla\!\cdot(\lambda\cdot\nabla T)
= -H_{vap}\dot m_{vap} + H_{dehyd}\dot m_{dehyd}.
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
 
\subsection{TH coupling mechanisms}
Equations~\eqref{eq:air}--\eqref{eq:energy} are \emph{two-way coupled}:
 
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Temperature drives the hydric field.} It enters through
  \emph{mass source terms} --- evaporation $\dot m_{vap}$ and dehydration
  $\dot m_{dehyd}$ are thermally activated --- and through
  \emph{temperature-dependent transport coefficients}: permeability $K(T)$,
  porosity $\phi(T)$, viscosities $\mu(T)$, saturation vapour pressure
  $p_{vs}(T)$ and diffusivity $D(T)$.
  \item \textbf{The hydric field drives the temperature.} The bulk flow of
  water and gas \emph{advects} enthalpy (the advection term in
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:energy}), while phase changes act as heat sinks
  ($-H_{vap}\dot m_{vap}$, $+H_{dehyd}\dot m_{dehyd}$), and the moisture
  content modifies $\rho C_p$ and $\lambda$.
\end{itemize}
 
The momentum balance and the resulting mechanical response are introduced in
Section~\ref{sec:mech}.


\section{Improved thermo-hydro-chemical (THC) model} \label{sec:thcmodel}

\subsection{Motivation}
In the TH model above, dehydration enters only as a prescribed source term $\dot m_{dehyd}(T)$ and the porosity follows a simple linear law $\phi=\phi_0+A_\phi(T-T_0)$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:phi-linear} in Appendix~\ref{app:hydric}). This treatment ignores three facts that strongly influence spalling: (i) the \emph{chemical state} of the cement paste before the accident depends on its hydration/curing history; (ii) the release of chemically bound water from the C--S--H is the chemical process that controls porosity, permeability and strength at high temperature; and (iii) chemical damage is essentially \emph{irreversible}. The improved model adopted here, following Scium\`e, Moreira and Dal~Pont \cite{sciume2024}, resolves the chemical field explicitly and couples it to the thermal and hydric fields.

\begin{draftblock}
\subsection{Chemical state variables}
The chemical state of the cement paste is described by the progress of two reactions:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{U}_{C} = \{\, \Gamma_{hydr},\; \Gamma_{dehyd}\,\},
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma_{hydr}\in[0,1]$ is the \emph{hydration degree} (formation of C--S--H during curing and ageing) and $\Gamma_{dehyd}\in[0,1]$ is the \emph{dehydration degree} (release of bound water at high temperature). Their evolution is governed by chemo-kinetic laws driven by time, temperature and relative humidity \cite{sciume2024}:
\begin{equation}
  \dot\Gamma_{hydr} = \mathcal{A}_{h}(\Gamma_{hydr},T,\mathrm{RH}),
  \qquad
  \dot\Gamma_{dehyd} = \mathcal{A}_{d}(\Gamma_{dehyd},T),
  \label{eq:chem-kinetics}
\end{equation}
so that the whole service life of the material --- from casting and curing, through ageing, up to the accident --- is represented within a single monolithic framework.
 
\subsection{Chemical coupling to the hydric field}
The dehydration reaction releases bound water, which becomes a mass source for the liquid/vapour phase. The dehydration rate that appears on the right-hand sides of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:water} and \eqref{eq:energy} is now obtained \emph{consistently} from the chemical kinetics rather than imposed empirically:
\begin{equation}
  \dot m_{dehyd} = -\,m_{w}^{0}\,\dot\Gamma_{dehyd},
  \label{eq:mdehyd-chem}
\end{equation}
where $m_w^{0}$ is the maximum mass of chemically bound water per unit volume that can be released by full dehydration.
 
\subsection{Chemo-physical constitutive couplings}
The key improvement is that the transport and mechanical properties are made explicit functions of the chemical state. Following Powers' stoichiometric model \cite{powers,sciume2024}, the volume fractions of the solid phases and the porosity evolve during both hydration and dehydration:
\begin{equation}
  \phi = \phi\big(\Gamma_{hydr},\Gamma_{dehyd}\big),
  \label{eq:phi-chem}
\end{equation}
which replaces the linear law $\phi=\phi_0+A_\phi(T-T_0)$. The intrinsic permeability and the tensile strength likewise degrade with the chemical state,
\begin{equation}
  K = K_0\,g_K\!\big(\Gamma_{dehyd},\,\mathcal{D}\big),
  \qquad
  f_t = f_{t,0}\,g_f\!\big(\Gamma_{dehyd},T\big),
  \label{eq:KF-chem}
\end{equation}
so that dehydration increases permeability (easing pressure relief) while lowering the strength that resists spalling, as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spalling}; $\mathcal{D}$ is the mechanical damage (Section~\ref{sec:mech}). The irreversibility of chemical damage is enforced through an adsorption--desorption description of the pore microstructure \cite{sciume2024}, so that re-wetting does not restore the dehydrated material.
 
\subsection{Resulting coupled system}
Collecting the primary unknowns, the improved model solves $\mathcal{U}_{THC}=\{p_g,p_c,T,\Gamma_{hydr},\Gamma_{dehyd}\}$ through the mass and energy balances \eqref{eq:air}--\eqref{eq:energy}, in which the source term~\eqref{eq:mdehyd-chem} and the chemo-physical couplings~\eqref{eq:phi-chem}--\eqref{eq:KF-chem} now close the system. A notable practical advantage of this formulation is that it requires only a limited number of experiments for calibration \cite{sciume2024}. The calibration against the Kalifa benchmark \cite{kalifa2000} and the numerical implementation are addressed in Chapter~\ref{ch:experiment}.

\end{draftblock}

\section{Coupling to the mechanical field} \label{sec:mech}

The mechanical response is driven by the converged TH/THC fields through a \emph{one-way} (staggered) coupling: at each time step the fields $(T,p_g,p_c)$ enter the mechanical problem as prescribed loading, and the resulting damage $\mathcal{D}$ feeds back weakly to the transport properties via Eq.~\eqref{eq:KF-chem} at the next step. The TH/THC fields act through two routes:
 
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Free strains.} The total strain is decomposed as
  $\bm{\varepsilon}=\bm{\varepsilon}_\sigma+\bm{\varepsilon}_{th}
  +\bm{\varepsilon}_{sh}$, where $\bm{\varepsilon}_{th}$ is the thermal
  dilation (driven by $T$) and $\bm{\varepsilon}_{sh}$ the capillary
  shrinkage (driven by $p_c$). Creep is neglected, so
  $\bm{\varepsilon}_\sigma=\bm{\varepsilon}_{el}$.
  \item \textbf{Pore-pressure loading.} Through Bishop's effective stress
  $\bm{\sigma}'=\bm{\sigma}^{Total}+\alpha\,p_s\,\mathbf{I}$, the average pore
  pressure $p_s=p_g-S_l p_c$ enters the load vector as an external loading;
  its build-up shifts $\bm{\sigma}'$ towards tension and promotes cracking
  when $f_t(T)$ is exceeded.
\end{itemize}
 
Together with the spalling criterion~\eqref{eq:spalling}, this closes the physical loop between the transport and mechanical fields. The constitutive model (linear elasticity coupled with Mazars continuum damage \cite{mazars1986}), its fracture-energy regularisation \cite{hillerborg1976}, and the spatial/temporal discretisation of the coupled problem are reported in full in Appendix~\ref{app:mechanical}; only the elements needed to interpret the results of the following chapters are retained here.
