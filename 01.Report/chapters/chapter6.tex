\section{Main findings}
The research conducted in this study has led to several key findings:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Implementation of the THC model:} The improved Thermo-Hydro-Chemical (THC) model \cite{sciume2024}, which explicitly incorporates the effective hydration degree $\tilde{\Gamma}$ as an internal state variable, was successfully implemented and executed in \textsc{Cast3M}. This approach elegantly replaced empirical temperature-dependent multipliers with a strictly chemo-physical description of porosity and permeability evolution.
    \item \textbf{Thermal calibration:} The thermal response of the model was accurately calibrated. By carefully balancing the boundary convective/radiative heat exchange and the bulk conduction parameters (heat capacity and thermal conductivity), the model achieved excellent agreement with the experimental data of Kalifa et al. \cite{kalifa2000} and the numerical benchmark of Dauti \cite{dauti2018} at all depths.
    \item \textbf{Gas-pressure ambiguity:} Despite an extensive calibration sweep involving the intrinsic permeability, hydration coupling coefficients, and boundary mass-transfer conditions, the deep-zone gas pressure could not be accurately reproduced. The model predicted a decay in pressure with depth, contradicting the experimental measurements. This discrepancy strongly suggests that the embedded pressure gauges may alter the local porous network, creating cavities that do not reflect the true intact pore pressure.
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity and dominant parameters:} A comprehensive stochastic assessment using \textsc{OpenTURNS} allowed for the variance-based sensitivity analysis of the model. The extraction of first- and second-order Sobol indices identified the intrinsic permeability $K_0$ as the absolute dominant parameter governing the magnitude of the pore pressure, while mix-composition uncertainties presented negligible interaction effects on the pressure peaks.
    \item \textbf{Reliability and spalling risk:} By propagating the material uncertainties, the reliability analysis successfully reconstructed the Probability Density Functions (PDFs) of the structural response. It quantified the probability of failure ($P_f$) and the risk of spalling based on a critical pressure threshold at the $10~\mathrm{mm}$ layer, confirming that deterministic approaches alone are insufficient for safety assessment.
    \item \textbf{Impact of spatial heterogeneity:} The investigation into the spatial variability of permeability using random fields (Turning Bands Method) revealed that assuming a homogeneous material property dangerously underestimates the structural risk. Localized weak zones trigger more severe capillary shrinkage and pressure localizations compared to the averaged homogeneous baseline.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Model limitations}
While the presented framework provides a deep insight into concrete behavior at high temperatures, several limitations remain:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Absence of mechanical validation:} Due to the limited scope of the internship, and because the reference Kalifa experiment only provides temperature and gas pressure data, the mechanical (M) behaviour could not be rigorously validated. Although the staggered mechanical coupling is theoretically established (\cref{chapter2}, \cref{appendixA}) and designed as an independent module that is easy to plug in, the numerical model currently validated and utilized in this work is strictly a TH(C) model.
    \item \textbf{Computational cost of random fields:} Fully integrating the \texttt{ALEA} random-field generator into the highly non-linear TH(C) model requires solving the system for dozens of spatial realizations. This computational bottleneck restricted the spatial heterogeneity study to a simplified isothermal poro-mechanical cube rather than the full Kalifa test.
    \item \textbf{Unresolved gas-pressure calibration:} As noted in the findings, the inability to match the gas pressure at deeper layers without violating the mathematical stability of the solver or the physical bounds of the parameters remains an open issue.
\end{itemize}

\section{Perspectives for future research}
Building upon the limitations and findings of this work, several avenues for future research are identified:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Non-intrusive experimental measurements:} To resolve the ambiguity of the gas-pressure measurements, future experimental campaigns should redesign the gauge setups or rely on non-intrusive techniques, such as \emph{in-situ} neutron tomography \cite{dauti2018}, to verify the actual moisture and pressure distribution without disturbing the concrete matrix.
    \item \textbf{Fully coupled 2D/3D THM formulation:} The mechanical framework should be extended to a fully coupled two-way system in 2D and 3D, where the mechanical damage $\mathcal{D}$ directly and dynamically updates the intrinsic permeability field during the simulation, capturing the drastic transport acceleration caused by micro-cracking.
    \item \textbf{Advanced surrogate modelling:} To make the stochastic propagation of random fields computationally tractable, advanced metamodels (e.g., Polynomial Chaos Expansions or neural networks) should be developed. This would allow replacing the expensive Monte Carlo simulations of the full THM solver with rapid analytical evaluations.
    \item \textbf{Full service-life simulation:} Following the complete vision of Sciumè et al. \cite{sciume2024}, the THC formulation should be expanded to simulate the entire lifespan of the structure continuously---from early-age curing, through long-term aging and self-desiccation, up to the accidental fire scenario.
    \item \textbf{Broader verification:} Finally, the robustness of the model should be verified against a wider range of concrete mixtures (e.g., ordinary concrete, fiber-reinforced concrete) and under more severe heating scenarios, such as the ISO 834 or hydrocarbon fire curves.
\end{itemize}
