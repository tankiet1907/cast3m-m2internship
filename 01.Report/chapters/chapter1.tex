\section{Context and problem statement}
Concrete containment structures under severe-accident thermal loading present coupled thermo-hydro-mechanical challenges \cite{dauti2018}.

\subsection{Concrete behaviour under high temperature} 
Main contextes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Concrete in nuclear containment structures is exposed to extreme temperatures during severe accidents.
    \item Rapid heating leads to strong thermal stresses, dehydration, cracking and possible spalling govern structural integrity.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Problem statement}

Main problems:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Complex multi-physics coupling under high temperature.
  \item Temperature- and damage-dependent evolution of transport properties with cracking and stiffness degradation altering the permeability and pore pressure.
  \item Reliable prediction requires a consistent thermo-hydro-mechanical (THM) framework.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations of existing THM models}
Main limitations of existing THM models:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Lack of fully coupled interactions between the damage state and transport properties. 
    \item Lack of consistent regularization for strain localization.
    \item Limited probabilistic assessment of parameter uncertainty and sensitivity.
\end{itemize}

\section{Objectives and scope of the thesis}
Main objectives of the thesis:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Understanding and Developing a THM model
    \item Temperature- and damage-dependent evolution of stiffness and transport properties.
    \item Numerical implementation and numerical validation.
    \item Probabilistic sensitivity analysis: Evaluating parameter uncertainty and identifying the dominant physical parameters.
\end{itemize}

The scope of this work is limited to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Thermal boundaries limited by severe accident conditions.
    \item Continuum-scale THM modelling by Cast3M.
    \item Damage modelling including temperature-dependent stiffness and transport properties.
    \item Probabilistic sensitivity analysis at the material parameter level.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research enviroment and Computational tools}

\subsection{3SR Laboratory}
The host institution providing the scientific environment, experimental data, and expertise in structural mechanics and risk analysis.

\subsection{Finite element code - Cast3M}
Cast3M (or Castem) is an open-source finite-element code developed at CEA, which is used to implement advanced constitutive laws and custom multiphysics formulations.  

Compared with COMSOL Multiphysics, Cast3m typically offers greater transparency and control for implementing tightly coupled THM while COMSOL offers a user-friendly GUI for many built-in multiphysics interfaces.

\subsection{Probabilistic analysis tool - Persalys}
Persalys is the computational tool dedicated to managing parameter uncertainty in material properties.

It is used to perform the probabilistic sensitivity analysis, generate random sampling (e.g., Monte Carlo simulations), and rank the dominant THM parameters.
